# Entire defect arithmetic and inverses of unbounded local variation

30 September 2026. This note concerns the established **local Cartesian
completion** $A=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}$, with its existing operations
and polynomially weighted rooted-ball seminorms. Spectra are taken in
the complexification $A_{\mathbb C}$. The real statements follow by
uniqueness and conjugation.

Let $E=\lim_{n\to\infty}(P_n-C_n)$ be the
[cut-line defect](SPARSE_DEFECTS_AND_RELATIVE_HEAT.md). The conclusions are
exact:

- $\sigma_{A_{\mathbb C}}(E)=\mathbb C$, and $1-tE$ is a nonunit for
  every nonzero real or complex $t$.
- In the complexification, the closed subalgebra generated by $E$ is a
  copy of the entire-function algebra $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$, with its
  compact-open Fréchet topology.
- Its ambient units are exactly the zero-free entire functions of $E$.
  In particular, $\exp(tE)$ and $\exp(-tE)$ are mutually inverse elements
  of unbounded local variation whenever $t\ne0$.
- Ambient divisibility between entire functions of $E$ is characterized
  exactly by the multiplicities of their complex zeros.

The function-algebra ingredients are classical. The work below identifies
the local geometric coordinates that make them exact statements about
this particular graph completion. No priority claim is made.

## 1. The path atoms of a cut

Fix $r\ge2$. Let $A_{r,j}$, for $0\le j<r$, be the rooted path with
arm lengths $j$ and $r$, and let $L_r$ have arm lengths $r,r$. The root
of $A_{r,0}$ is an endpoint. The already established local formula is

$$a_r:=T_r(E)=2\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\delta_{A_{r,j}}-2r\delta_{L_r},
\qquad \|a_r\|_1=4r.\tag{1}$$

The radius-zero marginal is zero. The same path formula holds at radius
one, but the independent coordinates below require radius at least two.

## 2. Explicit additive coordinates on the whole local monoid

For any rooted radius-$r$ ball $B$, let $d(B)$ be its root degree and
$Q(B)$ the number of simple four-cycles through the root. These cycles
need not be induced. All their vertices lie within distance two, so
$Q$ is visible at every radius under consideration.

Every four-cycle through the root of a Cartesian product either lies
in one factor or is the rectangle determined by one incident edge from
each factor. Thus, in the truncated local monoid,

$$d(B\star_r C)=d(B)+d(C),$$
$$Q(B\star_r C)=Q(B)+Q(C)+d(B)d(C).\tag{2}$$

Consequently

$$N(B)=d(B)-\binom{d(B)}2+Q(B)
=\frac{3d(B)-d(B)^2+2Q(B)}2\tag{3}$$

is additive. It equals one on each of the $r+1$ path atoms in (1).
On arbitrary balls $N$ need not be nonnegative; only additivity will
be needed for the character construction.

Let the sphere polynomial be

$$S_B(z)=\sum_{k=0}^r |\{v:d_B(o,v)=k\}|z^k
\pmod {z^{r+1}}.\tag{4}$$

Its constant term is one. Cartesian distances add, giving
$S_{B\star_r C}=S_BS_C\pmod {z^{r+1}}$. Its formal logarithm, computed
only through degree $r$, is therefore additive. Put

$$F(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z},\qquad
U_B(z)=\log S_B(z)-N(B)\log F(z)\pmod {z^{r+1}}.\tag{5}$$

For $0\le j<r$, define

$$\ell_j(z)=\log\left(1-\frac{z^{j+1}}{1+z}\right)
\pmod {z^{r+1}}.\tag{6}$$

The leading term of $\ell_j$ is $-z^{j+1}$, so these polynomials form
a triangular basis of the zero-constant polynomials through degree $r$.
There are unique rational-valued functions $c_j$ such that

$$U_B(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}c_j(B)\ell_j(z),\qquad
c_L(B)=N(B)-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}c_j(B).\tag{7}$$

Equations (2)–(7) show that all $c_j,c_L$ are additive on the entire
local monoid, not only on products of the path atoms. For an explicit
recursive implementation,

$$c_j(B)=-[z^{j+1}]
\left(U_B(z)-\sum_{i<j}c_i(B)\ell_i(z)\right).\tag{8}$$

The sphere polynomials of the path atoms are

$$S_{L_r}=F,\qquad
S_{A_{r,j}}=F\left(1-\frac{z^{j+1}}{1+z}\right)
\pmod {z^{r+1}}.\tag{9}$$

Since $N=1$ on all these atoms, their coordinates are exactly

$$c_i(A_{r,j})=\delta_{ij},\quad c_L(A_{r,j})=0,
\qquad c_i(L_r)=0,\quad c_L(L_r)=1.\tag{10}$$

Thus the exponent tuple of any local Cartesian product of the atoms
is recovered by these additive coordinates. In particular, distinct
tuples give nonisomorphic rooted product balls.

## 3. The spectrum is the whole complex plane

For arbitrary real phases $\theta_0,\ldots,\theta_{r-1},\theta_L$,
the function

$$s_\theta(B)=\exp\left(i\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\theta_jc_j(B)
                         +i\theta_Lc_L(B)\right)\tag{11}$$

is a bounded semicharacter on the whole local monoid. The possibly
negative or nonintegral values of the coordinates cause no problem:
the exponential has modulus one and respects their additivity. Hence

$$\chi_{r,\theta}(X)=\sum_B a_r^X(B)s_\theta(B)\tag{12}$$

is a continuous character of $A_{\mathbb C}$, bounded by
$\|T_rX\|_1$. It is also a character of the local convolution algebra.

Assign every $A_{r,j}$ the same unit-modulus value $u$ and assign $L_r$
the unit-modulus value $v$. Equation (10) permits these choices
independently, and (1) gives

$$\chi_{r,\theta}(E)=2r(u-v).\tag{13}$$

Differences of two points of the unit circle fill the closed disk of
radius two. For example, if $\lambda=\rho e^{i\phi}$ with
$0<\rho\le4r$, set $a=\rho/(4r)$ and take

$$u=e^{i\phi}(a+i\sqrt{1-a^2}),\qquad
v=e^{i\phi}(-a+i\sqrt{1-a^2});\tag{14}$$

then (13) equals $\lambda$. For $\lambda=0$, take $u=v=1$.
The local spectral upper bound is $\|a_r\|_1=4r$. We have therefore
proved the exact local result

$$\boxed{\sigma_{\ell^1(M_r)}(T_rE)=\{\lambda:|\lambda|\le4r\}}
\qquad(r\ge2).\tag{15}$$

The [global local-spectrum criterion](CHARACTERS_AND_INVERSION.md)
or the explicit characters (12) now gives

$$\boxed{\sigma_{A_{\mathbb C}}(E)=\mathbb C.}\tag{16}$$

For any $t\ne0$, choose $r\ge2$ with $4r\ge1/|t|$ and a character
having $\chi(E)=1/t$. Then $\chi(1-tE)=0$, ruling out an inverse.
This is a concrete finite-radius obstruction even when $|t|$ is small.

## 4. Exact power norms and the failed geometric reciprocal

Every product of $n$ atoms has $N=n$. Distinct powers of $E$ therefore
have disjoint local supports. Within a fixed power, (10) recovers each
atom's multiplicity; equivalently, the coefficient sign is determined
by the multiplicity $c_L$ of the sole negative atom. Consequently no
sign cancellation occurs in the multinomial expansion. For every finite
coefficient sequence,

$$\boxed{\left\|T_r\left(\sum_n a_nE^n\right)\right\|_1
=\sum_n|a_n|(4r)^n}\qquad(r\ge2).\tag{17}$$

The formal reciprocal of $1-ta_r$ is
$\sum_{n\ge0}t^n a_r^{*n}$. It is coefficientwise meaningful because
each atom has positive root degree, so only finitely many powers affect
any fixed rooted type. Formal inversion is unique. Equation (17) gives

$$\left\|(1-ta_r)^{-1}\right\|_1
=\frac1{1-4r|t|}\quad\text{if }4r|t|<1,\tag{18}$$

and divergence of the formal reciprocal's absolute coefficient sum
when $4r|t|\ge1$. Thus local invertibility holds exactly below this
radius-dependent threshold. No nonzero $t$ satisfies it at every radius.

## 5. The closed generated algebra is the entire-function algebra

Each atom has maximum degree at most two. A product of $n$ atoms has
degree at most $2n$, and its radius-$r$ ball has at most
$(r+1)(1+2n)^r$ vertices. Together with (17), this yields

$$p_{r,k}(E^n)\le(r+1)^k(1+2n)^{rk}(4r)^n
\qquad(r\ge2,k\ge1).\tag{19}$$

The smaller radii are dominated by radius two. If
$f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n$ is entire, the series

$$f(E)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nE^n\tag{20}$$

therefore converges in every defining seminorm. Its product respects
ordinary multiplication of entire functions. The same estimates give

$$\|T_r f(E)\|_1=\sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|(4r)^n,\tag{21}$$
$$p_{r,k}(f(E))\le(r+1)^k
\sum_{n\ge0}|a_n|(1+2n)^{rk}(4r)^n.\tag{22}$$

Polynomial factors in $n$ are absorbed by choosing a larger exponential
radius. Hence the induced topology on coefficients is exactly that
given by $\sum_n|a_n|R^n$ for every $R>0$. This is the usual compact-open
topology of $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)$: the supremum on the disk of radius
$R$ is at most that coefficient sum, while Cauchy's coefficient bound
on a larger disk of radius $S>R$ gives

$$\sum_n|a_n|R^n\le
\frac{\sup_{|z|\le S}|f(z)|}{1-R/S}.\tag{23}$$

Conversely, if polynomial expressions in $E$ converge in $A$, (17)
makes their coefficient sequences Cauchy in every one of these
exponentially weighted norms. The common limit is the coefficient
sequence of an entire function, and (22) identifies the graph limit
with (20). We have proved

$$\boxed{\overline{\mathbb C[E]}\cong\mathcal O(\mathbb C)}\tag{24}$$

as topological algebras. Over the reals take the entire functions with
real Taylor coefficients. In particular, (20) is injective and its
image is closed.

An additional distinction follows directly from (21):

$$f(E)\text{ has bounded local total variation}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f\text{ is constant}.\tag{25}$$

If some $a_n\ne0$ with $n\ge1$, its single contribution
$|a_n|(4r)^n$ grows without bound. By the established signed-measure
characterization, the intersection of this entire-function subalgebra
with the finite signed graph-measure model consists exactly of scalars.

## 6. Exact units and ambient divisibility

Continuity of (12) gives $\chi_{r,\theta}(f(E))=f(\chi_{r,\theta}(E))$.
If $f$ has any complex zero, a character from Section 3 consequently
annihilates $f(E)$ and rules out an ambient inverse. If $f$ is zero-free,
$1/f$ is entire and $(1/f)(E)$ is an inverse. Thus

$$\boxed{f(E)\in A_{\mathbb C}^{\times}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f\text{ has no zero in }\mathbb C,}
\tag{26}$$

even when the inverse is allowed to lie anywhere in the full local
Cartesian completion. For real coefficients the reciprocal remains
real. Applying (26) to $\lambda-f$ proves the exact spectral formula

$$\boxed{\sigma_{A_{\mathbb C}}(f(E))=f(\mathbb C).}\tag{27}$$

Every zero-free entire function has the form $f(0)e^g$ with entire
$g$ satisfying $g(0)=0$: integrate $f'/f$ on the simply connected plane.
For real Taylor coefficients, this normalized $g$ also has real Taylor
coefficients. Consequently all units of this subalgebra are precisely

$$f(0)\exp(g(E)),\qquad f(0)\ne0,\quad g(0)=0.\tag{28}$$

**Ambient divisibility theorem.** Let $f,g$ be entire and $f$ not
identically zero. Then

$$g(E)\in f(E)A_{\mathbb C}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g/f\text{ extends to an entire function}.
\tag{29}$$

Equivalently, every zero of $f$ must occur in $g$ with at least the
same multiplicity. When this holds, the unique ambient quotient is
$(g/f)(E)$, and hence remains in the entire-function subalgebra.

**Proof.** Suppose $f(E)Y=g(E)$. For every $|\lambda|\le4r$ with
$f(\lambda)\ne0$, choose a radius-$r$ character with $\chi(E)=\lambda$.
Then

$$\left|\frac{g(\lambda)}{f(\lambda)}\right|
=|\chi(Y)|\le\|T_rY\|_1.\tag{30}$$

Every potential pole of $g/f$ lies inside some such disk. Its uniform
bound (30) makes the singularity removable. The quotient $h=g/f$ is
therefore entire, and $h(E)$ supplies a solution. The established
integral-domain theorem gives uniqueness, so $Y=h(E)$. The converse
follows immediately by multiplication of entire functions. For real
$f,g$, the removable quotient has real coefficients as well.

## 7. Explicit units beyond finite signed measures

For $t\in\mathbb C$, the exponential series gives

$$\exp(tE)\exp(-tE)=1,\qquad
\boxed{\|T_r\exp(tE)\|_1=e^{4r|t|}}\quad(r\ge2).\tag{31}$$

For $t\ne0$, both the unit and its inverse have unbounded local
variation. Each individual radius still has finite weighted moments by
(22). The growth across radii is exactly what excludes a finite signed
graph-measure representation; it does not obstruct membership or
multiplication in the local Cartesian completion.

Equation (27) also gives

$$\sigma_{A_{\mathbb C}}(\exp(tE))=\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}
\qquad(t\ne0).\tag{32}$$

This is an explicit nonclosed spectrum in the Fréchet algebra. The
element is invertible even though zero is a limit point of its spectrum.
No Banach-algebra compact-spectrum assertion applies to the unrestricted
completion.

Every nonconstant polynomial in $E$ is a nonunit, by the fundamental
theorem of algebra and (26). Its zero-free entire functions supply the
completed units instead. For example, the formal geometric reciprocal
of $1-tE$ fails at sufficiently large radius, whereas factorial decay
in $\exp(tE)$ gives convergence at every radius and an explicit inverse.
These conclusions solve inversion on this closed subalgebra, rather
than on all elements with unbounded local variation.

## 8. Effective local approximation

For an entire function whose coefficients and tails are supplied
effectively, (22) provides a direct local certificate. In particular,
truncating the exponential after order $N$ gives, for every $r\ge1$,

$$p_{r,k}\left(\exp(tE)-\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{t^nE^n}{n!}\right)
\le(r+1)^k\sum_{n>N}\frac{(4r|t|)^n}{n!}(1+2n)^{rk}.
\tag{33}$$

At radius one this upper bound uses $\|T_1E\|_1=4$ and submultiplicativity;
the disjoint-support argument is needed only for the exact equalities
at $r\ge2$. The radius-zero exponential marginal is exactly the unit.

This is a factorial tail times a fixed polynomial. If
$a=4r|t|$, $m=rk$, and $N+2>a(1+2/(2N+3))^m$, successive terms after
the first omitted term have ratio at most

$$q_N=\frac{a}{N+2}\left(1+\frac2{2N+3}\right)^m<1.\tag{34}$$

Both factors in this term ratio decrease with the index. Therefore

$$\text{right side of (33)}\le
\frac{(r+1)^k a^{N+1}(2N+3)^m}{(N+1)!(1-q_N)}.\tag{35}$$

For rational $t$, this certificate is rational and eventually meets
every positive tolerance. It certifies a local approximation to a unit
whose global variation is infinite. Practical cost still depends on
the number and complexity of the rooted product types needed at the
requested radius; the existence of the certificate is not a claim
that generic exact graph isomorphism is inexpensive.

## 9. Dependencies and limits

The proof uses the already established defect marginals, local Cartesian
convolution, integral-domain theorem, and local character criterion.
The new explicit additive coordinates are (3)–(8). The remaining
ingredients are finite formal logarithms, elementary entire-function
theory, and direct coefficient estimates; no external classification
theorem is needed.

For the classical entire-function algebra and its equivalent
exponentially weighted coefficient norms, see S. J. Bhatt and S. R.
Patel, [*On Fréchet algebras of power
series*](https://repository.ias.ac.in/59672/1/9_PUB.pdf), Bulletin of
the Australian Mathematical Society 66 (2002), 135–148, Example 1.4,
pp. 137–138. The graph-specific identification is (24), established by
the local path coordinates and estimates above; the whole graph
completion is not identified with that classical function algebra.

The entire-function subalgebra has not been proved to be a retract of
the whole completion, and no general reconstruction theorem for its
generator is asserted. Equation (29) is a division statement, not a
classification of all ambient algebraic extensions. General inversion
outside the classes treated here remains governed by the local
absolute-summability criterion.
