# The ghost edge in the local completion

1 October 2026. The earlier Graph Reals exploration defined a "unit graph
stub" and a "ghost edge" as limits, and attributed a quadratic identity and
an inverse to them. The first note in the [repository README](../../README.md)
and the correction note
[Ghosts and Phantoms](../../Ghosts_and_Phantoms__A_Correction_to_an_Exploratory_Framework_for_Completing_Graph_Arithmetic.pdf)
record that this was wrong. This note shows that one of the old routes to
the ghost edge converges in the local Cartesian graph completion, and the
other does not. It determines the ghost edge's exact arithmetic and places
it among the ghost and phantom elements of the completion.

The statements are mathematical. No physical interpretation is claimed,
and the ghost cosmology is not revived. Results are **Proposed** in the
sense of the
[development path](../../reviews/local-completion-2026-09-30/DEVELOPMENT_PATH.md):
proofs are written below but unreviewed. The finite inputs are checked by
[verify_ghost_edge.py](verify_ghost_edge.py), with results in
[ghost_edge_results.json](ghost_edge_results.json).

## Summary

- **The stub and the ghost edge.** The stub is the local line,
  U(ℤ) = lim C_n/n. The ghost edge is G = U(ℤ) − 1: vertex mass 0 and one
  edge per vertex.
- **Its arithmetic.** The closed algebra generated by U(ℤ) is the real form
  of A^∞(D̄), with U(ℤ) as the coordinate function. Hence G is transcendental,
  σ(U(ℤ)) = D̄ and σ(G) = D̄ − 1.
- **Every old identity fails.**
  - G² ≠ 2G.
  - The stub has no inverse.
  - The functional calculus does not collapse to affine expressions.
  - The ghost with unit edge count is not unique.
- **Unit threshold.** 1 + tG is a unit exactly when Re t < ½.
- **The routes split.** The complete-graph route (2/(n(n−1)))K_n has no
  limit: its vertex mass escapes to infinite degree. The old construction
  treated it as equivalent to the cycle route only because it measured
  vertex and edge counts alone.
- **Ghosts are never nilpotent.** Ghosts (vertex mass 0) and phantoms
  (vertex mass and edge count both 0) form closed ideals. Because the
  completion is an integral domain, no nonzero ghost is nilpotent, and the
  powers of a ghost from the second on are invisible to every first-order
  coordinate at the vertex-mass character.
- **The most ghost-like fact** is the persistent negative mass of the
  [positive-cone checkpoint](STRICT_POSITIVE_CONES.md) (Section 4).

## 1. The two old routes

The old definitions (`ghostedge.tex`) are

    S := lim (1/n) C_n,   v(S) = 1, e(S) = 1,
    E_∅ := S − K_1 = lim (2/(n(n−1))) K_n,   v(E_∅) = 0, e(E_∅) = 1.

The old pages also claimed:
- the quadratic identity E_∅² = 2E_∅;
- the inverse S⁻¹ = K_1 − E_∅;
- that "all smooth functions reduce to affine expressions" in E_∅;
- that E_∅ is "the unique ghost element with unit edge count, no vertices,
  and zero operator", where the operator came from the old graph–field
  embedding.

**The cycle route converges.** For n > 2r + 1 every rooted r-ball of C_n
is the centred path on 2r + 1 vertices. So T_r(C_n/n) is the point mass at
B_r(ℤ) for all large n, and C_n/n converges to the local line U(ℤ) in every
seminorm p_{r,k}
([LITERATURE_REVIEW](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md): "C_n/n converges to the local
line"). It has V(U(ℤ)) = 1 and one edge per vertex.

**The complete-graph route diverges.** Put s_n = (2/(n(n−1)))K_n. Every
root of K_n has the ball K_n, so T_r(s_n) = (2/(n−1)) δ_{K_n} for r ≥ 1.
Then:
- V(s_n) = 2/(n−1) → 0;
- e(s_n) = 1 for every n;
- p_{1,2}(s_n) = 2n²/(n−1) → ∞.

A convergent sequence in a Fréchet space is bounded, so (s_n) has no limit.
The mechanism is that the vertex mass escapes to infinite degree. In
unweighted ℓ¹ the radius-one marginals tend to zero while the edge count
stays 1, so no topology that makes both the radius-one marginals and the
edge count continuous can give this sequence a limit. The completion's
polynomial weights exclude exactly this behaviour. The old construction
called the two routes equivalent because it measured only vertex and edge
counts, which is the diagnosis in the README's first note.

## 2. The arithmetic of the stub and the ghost edge

Write G := U(ℤ) − 1.

**Powers.** By continuity of multiplication,
U(ℤ)^j = lim (C_n^{□j})/n^j = U(ℤ^j), the hypercubic lattice per vertex.
- Every root of ℤ^j has degree 2j.
- Its r-ball has size b_r(j) = Σ_i 2^i C(j,i) C(r,i). This is a polynomial
  in j of degree r, with b_1(j) = 2j + 1 and b_r(j) ≤ (2j + 1)^r.

**Proposition 1.** The map Σ c_j w^j ↦ Σ c_j U(ℤ)^j is a topological algebra
isomorphism from the real form of A^∞(D̄) onto the closed subalgebra
generated by U(ℤ).

*Proof.* For r ≥ 1 the balls B_r(ℤ^j) are distinct, since their root
degrees differ. So p_{r,k}(Σ c_j U(ℤ)^j) = Σ |c_j| b_r(j)^k, with no
cancellation. The bounds on b_r(j) give exactly the topology of the
rapidly decreasing sequences 𝒮₊ of
[MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS](MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS.md) (23), whose complex
form is A^∞(D̄). Multiplication is the Cauchy product, because
U(ℤ)^i U(ℤ)^j = U(ℤ)^{i+j}. This is the argument of MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS
§5 for K₂/2, with the hypercube replaced by the lattice. ∎

*Consequences.*
- U(ℤ) and G satisfy no nonzero polynomial identity.
- In particular G² − 2G = U(ℤ²) − 4U(ℤ) + 3 ≠ 0, so the old quadratic
  identity fails.
- The generated algebra is infinite-dimensional, so the functional calculus
  does not collapse to affine expressions.

**Proposition 2.** For f in A^∞(D̄), f(U(ℤ)) is a unit of the completion
exactly when f has no zero on D̄. Hence σ(U(ℤ)) = D̄ and
σ(G) = {w − 1 : |w| ≤ 1}.

*Proof.* If f has no zero on D̄, then 1/f lies in A^∞(D̄) and (1/f)(U(ℤ))
is the inverse. Conversely, let D be the degree generating map
([MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS](MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS.md) (24)). It is a
continuous unital homomorphism into A^∞(D̄), and D(U(ℤ)) = z² because every
root of the line has degree two. By continuity, D(f(U(ℤ)))(z) = f(z²).
If f(w₀) = 0 with |w₀| ≤ 1, choose z₀ with z₀² = w₀. Evaluation at z₀
composed with D is then a character vanishing on f(U(ℤ)), so f(U(ℤ)) is not
a unit. Taking f(w) = λ − w gives the spectra. ∎

*Consequences.*
- **The stub is not invertible**, because 0 ∈ D̄. The old inverse fails
  directly: U(ℤ)(2 − U(ℤ)) = 2U(ℤ) − U(ℤ²) ≠ 1.
- **G is not a unit.** Its function w − 1 vanishes at w = 1, the point that
  corresponds to the vertex-mass character, since V(U(ℤ)) = 1.
- **1 + tG is a unit exactly when Re t < ½.** Its function (1 − t) + tw
  vanishes at w₀ = (t − 1)/t, and |w₀| ≤ 1 exactly when Re t ≥ ½. The old
  stub is the case t = 1.
- **The unit-edge ghost is not unique.** The old operator condition has no
  counterpart in the completion. Without it, (U(ℤ²) − 1)/2 also has vertex
  mass 0 and edge count 1, and the ghosts with a given edge count form an
  affine space of infinite dimension.

## 3. Ghosts and phantoms in the completion

Let V be the vertex-mass character and e the edge count. The edge count is
continuous, |e(x)| ≤ ½ p_{1,1}(x). It is a point derivation at V:
e(xy) = V(x)e(y) + e(x)V(y). This holds for finite graphs and extends by
continuity.

Following the correction note:
- **Ghosts** are the closed ideal ker V.
- **Phantoms** are the closed ideal ker V ∩ ker e.

The completion refines this picture. The continuous point derivations at V
form an infinite-dimensional space. It includes the neighbour-link
component counts ([NONSPECTRAL_INTRINSIC §1](NONSPECTRAL_INTRINSIC.md)) and
the four-clique count Q₄
([GEOMETRY_VERSUS_SPECTRUM §3](GEOMETRY_VERSUS_SPECTRUM.md)). For any such
derivation D, D(gh) = V(g)D(h) + D(g)V(h) = 0 when g and h are ghosts. So a
product of two ghosts is invisible to every first-order coordinate at V,
not only to the edge count. The completion is an integral domain
([MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS](MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS.md)), so no nonzero
ghost is nilpotent. The correction note's downward filtration
regular ⊃ ghost ⊃ phantom therefore never reaches zero.

| Element | V | e | Remarks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G = U(ℤ) − 1, the old ghost edge | 0 | 1 | Transcendental; its closed algebra is A^∞(D̄), shifted |
| E = lim(P_n − C_n), the cut line | 0 | −1 | Generates the entire functions; spectrum ℂ; exp(tE) is a unit ([UNBOUNDED_VARIATION_INVERSION](UNBOUNDED_VARIATION_INVERSION.md)) |
| G^k and E^k for k ≥ 2 | 0 | 0 | Nonzero; annihilated by every point derivation at V |
| X = U(R) − U(S), rook minus Shrikhande | 0 | 0 | A phantom invisible to every spectral character, but Q₄(X) = ½ and the link-component derivations detect it |

In the degree picture, V is the point z = 1. Ghosts vanish there. The edge
count is one first-order coordinate at that point, and the old phantoms
vanish to first order in that single direction. The completion has
infinitely many first-order directions at V. That is why vertex and edge
counts cannot determine an element, which is the error the README's first
note identifies.

## 4. Persistent negative mass

[STRICT_POSITIVE_CONES](STRICT_POSITIVE_CONES.md) proves that some positive
mass-one elements need negative coefficient mass, bounded below, in every
finite signed approximation. Every finite description of such an object
must contain negative graph terms whose total mass does not vanish, although
the object itself is positive. This is the most ghost-like phenomenon in
the completion. As an analogy only: quantum field theory uses auxiliary
"ghost" and regulator fields that a consistent calculation requires but that
never appear as physical states. No physical claim is made.
