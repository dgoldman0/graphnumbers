# Arithmetic of a single deleted edge in the square lattice

30 September 2026. Let $A=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}$ be the established
local Cartesian graph completion. This note studies a single deleted
edge of $\mathbb Z^2$, rather than a complete Cartesian seam. Its limit
element will be denoted by $P$; finite path graphs retain their existing
notation $P_n$.

The two-dimensional affected-root growth changes local variation from
linear to quadratic; cycles separately affect the local atom geometry.
The closed algebra generated by $P$
is again the entire-function algebra. Its ambient spectrum is all of
$\mathbb C$, and zero-free entire functions give exactly its units in
the full graph completion. These assertions use a local-regularity face
of the rooted-ball monoid, without assuming free Cartesian factorization
of all the modified balls.

## 1. The planar defect and its exact local geometry

Let $\Lambda$ be the square lattice, let $e=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}$, and put
$\Lambda'=\Lambda\setminus e$. For the finite induced box
$G_n=\Lambda[[-n,n]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2]$, form

$$P^{(n)}=(G_n\setminus e)-G_n.$$

The radius-$r$ marginal of this difference stabilizes once $n\ge2r+1$.
Thus $P^{(n)}\to P$ in every original weighted local seminorm. The
element $P$ has maximum-degree bound four, mass zero, and one-edge edit
budget one.

To see stabilization and identify the affected roots, write a lattice
root as $(x,y)$. Deleting $e$ changes its induced radius-$r$ ball exactly
when

$$\min\{|x|,|x-1|\}+|y|\le r-1.\tag{1}$$

If (1) fails, a path of length at most $r$ cannot traverse $e$: the
nearer endpoint is at distance at least $r$, and the other endpoint is
one step farther away. Hence neither the ball's vertices nor its induced
edges change. If (1) holds, the nearer endpoint remains at distance
less than $r$ and has degree three after the deletion. Its full degree
is visible inside the ball, so the modified ball differs from the
intact grid ball. All these neighborhoods lie inside the stated buffered
box.

There are exactly

$$N_r=\sum_{|y|\le r-1}2(r-|y|)=2r^2\tag{2}$$

affected roots. For an explicit finite expression, let $B_r$ be the
intact lattice ball, and let $A_{r,h,b}$ be the ball of $\Lambda'$ rooted
at $(-h,b)$, for $h,b\ge0$ and $h+b\le r-1$. Reflection across the
deleted edge's midpoint and reflection across its row give

$$
T_rP=\sum_{h+b\le r-1}w_b\,\delta_{A_{r,h,b}}
        -2r^2\delta_{B_r},\qquad
w_0=2,\quad w_b=4\ (b>0).\tag{3}
$$

The formula allows positive terms with isomorphic rooted balls to merge.
No classification of those possible mergers is needed below. None can
merge with $B_r$, because of the visible degree-three interior vertex.
Therefore

$$\boxed{\|T_rP\|_1=4r^2}\qquad(r\ge1).\tag{4}$$

In particular $P$ has no finite signed-measure representation.

There is also an exact formula for every weighted seminorm. Put
$b_r=|B_r|=1+2r(r+1)$. If the root lies off the row containing $e$, all
its lattice distances remain unchanged: a shortest Manhattan path can
perform its horizontal part away from that row. Its modified ball has
$b_r$ vertices. A root $(-h,0)$ loses precisely those vertices across
the cut whose original distances are $r-1$ or $r$; the detour costs two
additional steps. Thus its ball has $b_r-2$ vertices if $h\le r-2$,
and $b_r-1$ vertices if $h=r-1$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{p_{r,k}(P)=
2(r-1)(b_r-2)^k+2(b_r-1)^k+(4r^2-2r)b_r^k.}\tag{5}
$$

This includes (4) at $k=0$. At radius zero the marginal is zero.

The degree generating function is $D(P)(z)=2(z^3-z^4)$, the same as
that of the Cartesian seam element $EL$. The elements differ: the
single-edge defect has variation $4r^2$, whereas the seam element has
variation $4r$. The latter follows by multiplying the cut-line atoms
by the line ball; their signs remain separated by visible degree
deficiency.

## 2. A local regularity face and growing spectral disks

For $r\ge1$, call a rooted ball $C$ locally regular when every vertex
at distance strictly less than $r$ from its root has the same degree
as the root. These degrees are measured inside $C$; all neighbors of
an interior vertex are present. Let $\mathrm{Reg}_r$ denote this set.

**Face lemma.** For the rooted Cartesian product monoid $M_r$,

$$C\star_r D\in\mathrm{Reg}_r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C,D\in\mathrm{Reg}_r.\tag{6}$$

**Proof.** At any interior product vertex $(u,v)$, the degree is
$\deg_C(u)+\deg_D(v)$. If both factors are locally regular this sum
is constant. Conversely, fixing $v$ at its root and varying an interior
$u$ shows that local regularity of the product forces local regularity
of $C$; reverse the roles for $D$. $\square$

Thus the indicator of $\mathrm{Reg}_r$ is a bounded multiplicative
function on $M_r$. For $|z|\le1$,

$$s_{r,z}(C)=\mathbf1_{\mathrm{Reg}_r}(C)z^{\deg(o_C)}\tag{7}$$

is a bounded semicharacter, with $0^0=1$. It defines a continuous
character of the graph completion through its radius-$r$ marginal.

For $r\ge2$, every positive atom in (3) is locally irregular. If its
root is an edge endpoint, the root has degree three and an interior
neighbor has degree four. Otherwise the root has degree four and the
nearer endpoint is an interior vertex of degree three. The intact ball
is locally regular of degree four. Therefore

$$\chi_{r,z}(P)=-2r^2z^4.\tag{8}$$

It follows that the local spectrum contains the closed disk of radius
$2r^2$. The norm bound (4) gives the other inclusion:

$$
\overline{\mathbb D}_{2r^2}
\subseteq\sigma_{\ell^1(M_r)}(T_rP)
\subseteq\overline{\mathbb D}_{4r^2}\qquad(r\ge2).\tag{9}
$$

Taking the union over radii proves

$$\boxed{\sigma_{A_{\mathbb C}}(P)=\mathbb C.}\tag{10}$$

In particular $1-tP$ is a nonunit for every nonzero real or complex
$t$. At an individual radius, $4r^2|t|<1$ suffices for an ordinary local
Neumann inverse, while $2r^2|t|\ge1$ certifies local noninvertibility by
(8). The gap between these general sufficient tests is intentional.

## 3. The closed entire calculus

The face lemma isolates one coefficient in every power of $P$. A product
containing a positive atom from (3) is locally irregular. A product of
$n$ negative intact atoms is the regular ball of $\mathbb Z^{2n}$ and
has coefficient $(-2r^2)^n$. Different $n$ have different root degrees.
Consequently, for any finite coefficient sequence and $r\ge2$,

$$
\sum_n|a_n|(2r^2)^n
\le\left\|T_r\!\left(\sum_n a_nP^n\right)\right\|_1
\le\sum_n|a_n|(4r^2)^n.\tag{11}
$$

No other coefficient is required to remain uncancelled. In particular,
(11) does not assert freeness of the full list of modified atoms.

The maximum degree in an $n$-fold product is at most $4n$. The weighted
upper bound is therefore

$$
p_{r,k}\!\left(\sum_n a_nP^n\right)
\le(r+1)^k\sum_n|a_n|(4r^2)^n(1+4n)^{rk}.\tag{12}
$$

The original topology induced on $\mathbb R[P]$ is exactly the
entire-function topology: the lower bound supplies arbitrarily large
coefficient radii, and the polynomial factor in (12) is absorbed by
any larger coefficient radius. Thus

$$f\longmapsto f(P)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nP^n\tag{13}$$

is a topological algebra isomorphism from real entire functions onto
$\overline{\mathbb R[P]}$. The same statement holds over $\mathbb C$.

Every evaluation $f\mapsto f(\lambda)$ extends to a character of $A_{\mathbb C}$:
choose $r$ and $z$ in (8) with $-2r^2z^4=\lambda$. Hence

$$
f(P)\in A_{\mathbb C}^{\times}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f\text{ has no zeros on }\mathbb C,
\qquad\sigma_{A_{\mathbb C}}(f(P))=f(\mathbb C).\tag{14}
$$

For necessity, a zero is detected by the extended evaluation. For
sufficiency, $1/f$ is entire and (13) supplies the inverse. A real
zero-free entire function has real reciprocal coefficients, giving the
real version as well.

For nonzero real $t$, $U_t=\exp(tP)$ and $U_{-t}$ are inverse units.
Equation (11) gives

$$e^{2r^2|t|}\le\|T_rU_t\|_1\le e^{4r^2|t|}\qquad(r\ge2).\tag{15}$$

Both units therefore lie outside the finite signed-measure model.
For numerical local approximation, put $\lambda=4r^2|t|$ and $m=rk$.
Truncation at power $N$ has the bound

$$
p_{r,k}\!\left(U_t-\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{t^nP^n}{n!}\right)
\le(r+1)^k\sum_{n>N}(1+4n)^m\frac{\lambda^n}{n!}.\tag{16}
$$

The summand ratios decrease. When
$\rho=\lambda(4N+9)^m/((N+2)(4N+5)^m)<1$, the right side is at most

$$
(r+1)^k\frac{(4N+5)^m\lambda^{N+1}}{(N+1)!(1-\rho)}.\tag{17}
$$

All these certificate quantities are rational for rational $t$.

## 4. Radius two has an exact free-atom calculation

The radius-two positive atoms have root positions $(0,0)$, $(-1,0)$,
and $(0,1)$, with coefficients $2,2,4$; the intact atom has coefficient
$-8$. Call the four atoms $A,B,C,D$, respectively.

Four additive local invariants distinguish their multiplicities. Let
$d$ be root degree, $Q$ the number of simple four-cycles through the
root, $s_2$ its distance-two sphere size, and put

$$N=(3d-d^2+2Q)/2,\quad L_2=s_2-d^2/2,\quad
J=\sum_{v\sim o}(\deg(v)-d)^2.$$

The invariants are additive under $\star_2$. Degree is additive;
$Q(X\star_2Y)=Q(X)+Q(Y)+d(X)d(Y)$ proves additivity of $N$.
Sphere generating functions multiply, so their formal logarithm
coefficient $L_2$ adds. Finally a neighbor changing the first coordinate
has degree difference $\deg_X(v)-d(X)$ from the product root, and the
second coordinate contributes its own such differences, proving the
formula for $J$.

Their values are

| Atom | $d$ | $N$ | $L_2$ | $J$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $A$ | 3 | 2 | $5/2$ | 3 |
| $B$ | 4 | 2 | $-1$ | 1 |
| $C$ | 4 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| $D$ | 4 | 2 | 0 | 0 |

This matrix has determinant $24$. Hence the four atoms are freely
independent in the commutative local monoid: equality of two products
forces equality of all four multiplicities. Linear combinations of
the four additive invariants can assign arbitrary phases to these
atoms; their exponentials are bounded semicharacters on the full local
monoid. The weighted sum of atom phases can fill the disk of radius
$16$ because the negative weight $8$ equals the sum $2+2+4$ of the
positive weights. Therefore

$$
\sigma_{\ell^1(M_2)}(T_2P)=\overline{\mathbb D}_{16},\qquad
\left\|T_2\!\left(\sum_n a_nP^n\right)\right\|_1
=\sum_n|a_n|16^n.\tag{18}
$$

In particular $\|T_2\exp(tP)\|_1=e^{16|t|}$ exactly. This local
freeness assertion concerns radius two only; (9), (11), and (15) are the
general-radius statements proved here.

### Why the cut-line sphere coordinates do not transfer unchanged

At radius three, consider the modified balls rooted at $(-1,-1)$ and
$(0,-2)$, for the same deleted edge $\{(0,0),(1,0)\}$. They are rooted
nonisomorphic. Both have 25 vertices, 35 edges, degree four at the root,
four root-containing four-cycles, and sphere vector $(1,4,8,12)$. The
intact lattice ball has the same root degree, four-cycle count, and
sphere vector, but has 36 edges. Thus all three receive the same
cut-line additive coordinates $(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_L)=(0,0,0,2)$ from
[the cut-line inversion note](UNBOUNDED_VARIATION_INVERSION.md).
Those coordinates cannot separate even these two positive planar atoms
from the negative intact atom. The local-regularity face does separate
their signs and is the reason the general-radius proof above works.
This collision does not prove that the planar atoms satisfy a product
relation; their full factorization remains a separate question.

## 5. Interpretation and verification scope

This example places a genuinely finite edge modification in a medium
with cycles inside the same entire-function arithmetic found for the
line cut. Geometry remains visible in its embedding: the marginal
variation grows quadratically, its character disks grow quadratically,
and its effective exponential tails use the planar degree and growth
constants. The abstract one-generator entire algebra does not by itself
distinguish these geometries.

The local regularity face and explicit radius-two additive invariants
supply the proofs. The library fixtures check buffered stabilization,
unweighted variation, and some low-radius products. The original verifier
does not directly check weighted formula (5), the determinant 24 of the
invariant table, or arbitrary signed polynomial coefficients. Those are
proof claims pending dedicated independent fixtures; finite checks do not replace
the arguments for arbitrary radii or global invertibility. No priority
claim or classification of all planar local-product relations is made.
