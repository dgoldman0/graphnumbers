Research assessment, 30 September 2026. Scope: the v0.1 construction in this

This review postdates the original proof note.

## Assessment

**Most of the mathematical ingredients already have established names and
substantial prior literature. No novelty claim is currently justified.** The
finite arithmetic is an existing graph ring. Local graph nearness is an existing
convergence theory. Completing submultiplicative seminorms and applying entire
power series are standard topological-algebra constructions.

The search did not locate the exact combination of Cartesian multiplication,
signed rooted-ball counting measures, all polynomial neighborhood weights, and
the particular closure used here. That is an unresolved bibliographic question.
Differences from the specific constructions below establish useful distinctions;
they do not establish a new mathematical contribution.

A defensible description of the present work is:

> A candidate locally multiplicatively convex completion of the classical
> Cartesian graph algebra, using weighted local-neighborhood distributions.

The accompanying [comparison lemmas](COMPARISON_LEMMAS.md) make the principal
identifications and distinguishing examples explicit. The [search record](SEARCH_LOG.md)
states what was searched, what was read, and the remaining coverage limits.

## The object being compared

Let C range over nonempty connected finite simple undirected graphs, up to
isomorphism. The initial real algebra is

$$A_0=\bigoplus_C\mathbb R[C],\qquad [C][D]=[C\square D].$$

An ordinary graph is the sum of its connected components; the empty graph is
zero and K1 is the multiplicative unit. Write a_r^x(B) for the signed coefficient
of rooted radius-r neighborhood type B in x. The topology is generated by

$$p_{r,k}(x)=\sum_B |V(B)|^k\,|a_r^x(B)|,
\qquad r\geq0,\quad k\geq1.$$

The candidate is the closure of the diagonal image of A0 in the corresponding
countable product of weighted convolution Banach algebras. The word *Cartesian*
means the usual graph product whose distances add, not merely a product having
a Cartesian vertex set.

## The closest graph-arithmetic sources

### The finite algebra is explicitly in the literature

Van de Woestijne [1] uses formal integer combinations of connected graph classes,
extends graph products bilinearly, and proves that the Cartesian graph ring is
isomorphic to a polynomial ring in countably many commuting variables. Thus,
after extending scalars,

$$A_0\cong\mathbb R[X_P:P\text{ is a connected Cartesian-prime graph}].$$

The prime-factorization input is the classical Sabidussi–Vizing theorem. Our
original proof avoided needing it, but avoiding it does not make the resulting
finite algebra different. The polynomial identification concerns A0; it does
not identify its chosen topological completion with a familiar function space.

Imrich, Klep and Smertnig [2] give a recent monoid-algebra treatment and extend
graph arithmetic to certain infinite disjoint unions using generalized formal
series. Their Section 3.1 also distinguishes unrestricted formal series in
infinitely many variables from an ideal-adic completion. Their infinite sums
are not automatically elements of our algebra: an unweighted sum of infinitely
many nonempty components has divergent vertex mass here.

### Knill already develops graph-number completions

Knill [3] discusses the weak/Cartesian graph ring as well as other graph rings.
His 2019 paper [4] explicitly proposes real and complex Banach completions of
graph arithmetic, using join and its compatible product. The 2021 treatment [5]
uses the complementary disjoint-union/strong-product presentation and discusses
weighted Wiener algebras and functional calculus. This is direct prior art for
the broad ambition of graphs extending ordinary numbers in a complete algebra.

Two calculations distinguish the current candidate from that Banach model:

- Multiplication: K2 Cartesian K2 is C4, whereas K2 strong K2 is K4.
- In the connected-component norm weighted by vertex count, distinct normalized
  cycles have distance 2. In our topology, C_n/n converges to the local line.

More decisively, our topology is nonnormable. Consequently it cannot be linearly
homeomorphic to a Banach algebra. This rules out topological equivalence with
Knill's stated Banach completion; a different product alone would not rule out
an abstract algebraic isomorphism.

## What the nearness already means

For a nonempty finite graph G, let U(G) be its uniform-root distribution on
connected rooted graphs, taking the component containing the sampled vertex.
The normalized coordinates T_r(G/|V(G)|) are exactly the radius-r marginals of
U(G). This is the empirical neighborhood distribution of [8].

With a common maximum-degree bound, only finitely many radius-r neighborhood
types are possible. Weighted l1 convergence on those types is just coordinate
convergence. Hence normalized graph convergence in our topology is precisely
Benjamini–Schramm convergence on that class. The normalized cycles and Cartesian
grids are standard local limits, not new limit objects [6–8].

For unbounded degrees, weak convergence alone does not control unbounded local
statistics. Kurauskas [9], Theorem 2.1, characterizes convergence of normalized
connected embedding counts on h vertices by convergence, equivalently uniform
integrability, of the root-degree moment of order h−1, assuming local weak
convergence and a finite limiting moment. Example 2.1 uses a small dense clique
to disrupt counts without changing the local weak limit. This is the established
phenomenon behind our triangle obstruction.

Our weights impose uniform integrability for every polynomial in every finite
neighborhood size. A comparison proved in [Lemma 3](COMPARISON_LEMMAS.md#3-positive-normalized-convergence)
shows that, for normalized finite-graph sequences with a locally finite limit,
this is equivalent to local weak convergence together with convergence of all
root-degree moments to their finite limiting values. This statement is an
inference proved here from standard moment bounds; [9] does not construct our
signed topological algebra.

The signed setting requires separate care. Cancellation occurs before absolute
values are taken in each local histogram. Results for positive probability
measures cannot simply be asserted for arbitrary signed completed elements.

## The completion in standard measure-algebra language

Associate to a finite graph its unnormalized rooted counting measure

$$\mu_G=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\delta_{[G(v),v]}.$$

Here G(v) is the connected component of v. Let rooted Cartesian product push
forward the product of two measures. Then

$$\mu_{G\sqcup H}=\mu_G+\mu_H,
\qquad \mu_{G\square H}=\mu_G*\mu_H.$$

Radius truncation gives T_r(G). Thus the candidate can be described exactly as a
weighted local convolution completion of these counting measures. This
reformulation is proved in [Lemma 1](COMPARISON_LEMMAS.md#1-counting-measures-and-convolution).
It explains why standard weighted semigroup algebras [10] and locally m-convex
algebras [11] supply the completion theorem.

That counting-measure calculation alone does not identify the completed
space. A bound at each radius does not give a uniform total-variation bound
over all radii. The subsequent [representation theorem](REPRESENTATION_THEOREM.md)
identifies the compatible arrays that belong to the completion and proves
that a global finite signed measure exists exactly when the local total
variations are uniformly bounded. An explicit cycle series fails that bound.

## Why the calculus is standard

Let V and E be the continuous vertex- and edge-count functionals. The identities

$$V(XY)=V(X)V(Y),\qquad
E(XY)=V(X)E(Y)+E(X)V(Y)$$

say that V is a character and E is a point derivation at V. Equivalently,

$$J(X)=V(X)+\varepsilon E(X),\qquad\varepsilon^2=0,$$

is a homomorphism into the dual-number algebra. For polynomials, and then by
continuity for entire power series,

$$E(f(X))=f'(V(X))E(X).$$

This follows from the ordinary dual-number chain rule. On finite graphs one can
also obtain it from the degree generating polynomial
D_G(z)=sum_v z^{deg(v)}: D_G(1)=V(G), D'_G(1)=2E(G), and Cartesian product
multiplies these polynomials. The polynomial itself is established; [13]
reviews its definition and earlier references. The identities above are
verified directly in [Lemma 4](COMPARISON_LEMMAS.md#4-the-edge-observable-is-a-point-derivation).

The real line, entire functional calculus, graph directions of polynomial maps,
and commutation of continuous linear observables with path differentiation and
integration remain useful properties. They follow from the algebra and locally
convex calculus [12]. They do not establish a new differential structure on
combinatorial edge edits, nor general inverse-function or evolution theorems.

The last two are especially relevant alternatives if the desired observables
concern information-theoretic capacity. Paper [20] was checked in its June 2026
revision. Paper [21] is a preprint dated 25 September 2026, five days before this
review; the search therefore includes work beyond the older graph-limit canon.

## A current obstruction to an overly broad representation claim

Limits of uniform-root finite graphs obey the mass-transport principle:
they are unimodular [7]. The converse finite-approximation conjecture is the
Aldous–Lyons conjecture. The papers of Bowen–Chapman–Lubotzky–Vidick and
Bowen–Chapman–Vidick [22–23] give a negative resolution. It would be incorrect to
identify the positive finite-graph limit class with all unimodular random rooted
graphs merely from local compatibility or mass transport.

This bears on the positive normalized closure. It does not itself classify the
positive elements obtainable as limits of arbitrary signed combinations in our
larger algebra. Those are different questions. No such identification is claimed
in the v0.1 note.

The later representation proof does identify the positive mass-one elements
of the signed completion with unimodular laws having all polynomial
neighborhood-size moments finite. Its approximants are signed combinations;
it makes no positive-approximation assertion.

The loss of global information is also familiar in local-limit theory.
Local-global convergence and graphings [24] provide a relevant comparison if
the project needs colored-neighborhood or global partition information.
Compatibility of that stronger framework with the present signed arithmetic
would require a new argument.

## What would count as progress now

The candidate currently provides a documented assembly of established ideas,
with a specific topology and worked obstructions. A changed presentation or a
different list of seminorms would not by itself make a meaningful contribution.
The review identified the following research directions. The first now has
a proof in the representation follow-up:

1. **Identify the completed space.** Characterize the allowed signed local
   marginals and give necessary and sufficient conditions for representation by
   a countably additive finite signed measure. Addressed in
   [REPRESENTATION_THEOREM.md](REPRESENTATION_THEOREM.md).
2. **Establish a universal or minimality property.** Determine whether the chosen
   topology is forced by Cartesian continuity and a specified class of
   observables, or whether substantially weaker choices do the same work.
3. **Prove an application beyond the inherited calculus.** For example, derive a
   quantitative approximation or stability theorem for a graph process using
   multiplication and the topology together.
4. **Resolve the intended geometry.** If global connectivity or dense graphon
   approximation is essential, the current topology fails those benchmarks and
   should be evaluated accordingly.

The responsible novelty statement remains: **the exact signed weighted local
completion was not found in this search; its originality has not been
established.** The arithmetic, real-scalar extension, local-limit perspective,
and basic calculus should be credited to their existing frameworks.

## Multiplication follow-up

The later [multiplication and units note](MULTIPLICATION_AND_UNITS.md)
establishes a domain theorem, a necessary-and-sufficient recursive unit
criterion, and a complemented analytic function subalgebra. It also proves
that nonunits are dense in this topology. These characterize additional
properties of the fixed construction; they do not establish priority or
a graph-theoretic application.

The proof uses cumulants to turn Cartesian multiplication into addition
of local invariants. Convolution logarithms and moment-cumulant relations
are established methods; see Ebrahimi-Fard and Patras,
[*Cumulants, free cumulants and half-shuffles*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.5664),
Sections 4, 7–8. The relation between Cartesian adjacency operators and
tensor independence is already discussed in [19]. The follow-up note
explicitly constructs its rooted edge-coloring coalgebra and proves the
ordering and reciprocal-balance arguments, rather than attributing those
particular results to these general references.

Whether these exact theorems or an equivalent completion already appear in
the literature remains unresolved. The targeted follow-up search is
recorded in SEARCH_LOG.md.
